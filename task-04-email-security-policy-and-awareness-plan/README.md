# Task 04 · Email security policy and awareness plan

**Program:** stc × Misk Virtual Work Experience, Cybersecurity Incident Response &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Deliverables:** Security policy, awareness campaign plan

## Task

After the previous incidents, the manager asked for an awareness plan for employees. To do that:

1. Write a new email security policy based on best practices. It must include the purpose, objectives, scope, responsibilities of all employees, responsibilities of information security staff, and the policy statements.
2. Include a policy for **sending emails**.
3. Include a policy for **opening attachments**.
4. Design an awareness plan covering the timeline, goal, awareness method and target audience.

## Overview

The earlier tasks showed how attacks actually reach a company: a phishing email with a fake pay slip, and an infostealer that reached its server over plain HTTP. Technical controls caught part of it, but the entry point each time was a person opening something. This task closes that loop. The policy sets clear rules for everyone, and the awareness plan makes sure employees actually know and follow them.

The policy is aligned with the **NCA Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC 2-4, email protection)** and Saudi Arabia's **Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)**.

### 1. Purpose

Email is one of the most important communication tools in the organization, and it is also one of the most common ways attackers spread malware and steal data through phishing emails and malicious attachments. This policy sets clear rules for using email securely and reducing the cybersecurity risks related to it, in line with the requirements of the National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) and the applicable regulations in Saudi Arabia.

### 2. Objectives

- **Confidentiality:** protect the data of the organization and its customers from unauthorized access or leakage through email.
- **Integrity:** make sure messages and data are only changed in a defined and authorized way.
- **Availability:** make sure authorized users have continuous access to the email service.
- Reduce the risk of phishing emails, malicious attachments and impersonation.
- Define responsibilities and explain acceptable and unacceptable email use.

### 3. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors and trainees, and any third party given an official email account.
- All email systems in the organization and all devices used to access them, including company devices, mobile phones and approved personal devices.
- All messages and attachments sent or received through the official email, inside and outside the organization.

### 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Phishing | A fake message that pretends to come from a trusted source to trick the user into opening a malicious link or attachment, or entering their data. |
| Malicious attachment | A file attached to an email that contains malware which runs when the file is opened. |
| Spoofing | Sending a message that looks like it comes from another person or organization in order to deceive. |
| Multi-factor authentication (MFA) | A login method that checks the user in two or more steps, such as a password and a code sent to a mobile phone. |
| Classified data | Any confidential or sensitive data according to the organization's data classification policy. |

### 5. Roles and responsibilities

**5.1 All employees**
- Read and understand this policy and sign the acknowledgment form.
- Use the official email for work purposes only.
- Protect the email password, never share it with anyone, and enable MFA.
- Do not open links or attachments from unknown or unexpected senders.
- Report any suspicious email or security incident to the information security team immediately.
- Attend the security awareness programs provided by the organization.

**5.2 Information security staff**
- Run email protection and filtering systems to block spam and phishing emails before they reach employees.
- Scan attachments and links in an isolated environment (sandbox) before they reach mailboxes.
- Enable sender domain authentication technologies (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) to prevent spoofing.
- Enable Data Loss Prevention (DLP) to stop classified data from leaving through email.
- Receive and quickly handle employee reports, and block malicious senders and websites that are discovered.
- Run regular awareness campaigns and phishing simulations, and measure compliance with this policy.
- Review and update this policy.

### 6. Policy statements

**6.1 General requirements**
- The official email belongs to the organization, and the organization may monitor messages when needed, following approved procedures and with prior authorization.
- Personal email accounts and unapproved external storage services must not be used for company work.
- The official email must not be used to sign up for websites or services that are not related to work.
- MFA must be enabled when accessing email from outside the organization.
- Emails are archived and backed up regularly according to the organization's procedures.

**6.2 Sending emails**
- Check the recipient's address before sending, especially when addresses are suggested automatically, and make sure the correct file is attached.
- Classified data, such as passwords, financial data and customer data, must not be sent unless it is encrypted and approved by the direct manager.
- Classify each email based on the importance of its content and attachments, according to the data classification policy.
- Use BCC when sending to a large number of external recipients to protect the privacy of their addresses.
- Automatic forwarding of work email to an external or personal email is not allowed.
- Sending chain letters, advertisements, or any offensive or illegal content is not allowed.
- Emails must be clear and professional and include an official signature with the sender's name and job title, with a disclaimer added to external emails.
- Any request by email to transfer money or change bank details must be verified by calling the requester directly before taking action.
- Large files should not be sent by email; use the organization's approved file sharing system instead.

**6.3 Opening attachments and links**
- Do not open any attachment or link from an unknown or unexpected sender.
- Check the full sender address, not just the display name, because attackers may impersonate a known person or organization.
- If you receive an unexpected attachment from a colleague or a known party, confirm it through another channel, such as a phone call, before opening it.
- Be careful with dangerous attachments such as executable files and password-protected compressed files; the email system blocks them automatically.
- Do not click "Enable Content" or run macros in files received by email unless the information security team approves.
- Hover over a link to make sure it leads to the correct website before clicking it.
- Never enter your username or password on a page opened from an attachment or a link inside an email.

**6.4 Reporting suspicious emails**
- If you suspect an email, do not open its attachments, click its links, or reply to it.
- Report it to the information security team immediately using the report button in the email client or the approved reporting channel, and do not forward it to colleagues.
- If you opened a suspicious attachment or link by mistake, disconnect the device from the network and report it to the information security team immediately. Do not try to fix the problem yourself.

### 7. Regulatory compliance

- The **Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC)** issued by the National Cybersecurity Authority, especially control 2-4 on email protection.
- The **Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)**, supervised by the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA), which prohibits sharing or sending the personal data of customers or employees without a legal basis.
- The organization's internal policies, such as the data classification policy and the acceptable use policy.

### 8. Review, compliance and violations

- The Cybersecurity Department reviews this policy at least once a year, or when there are changes in laws and regulations, in the organization's procedures, or when new risks appear.
- The Head of the Cybersecurity Department regularly checks compliance with this policy, and all personnel must comply with it.
- Any violation may lead to disciplinary action according to the organization's regulations, such as additional training, a warning, or stronger action depending on how serious the violation is.

## Part 2 · Awareness plan

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Duration** | One month (4 weeks) |
| **Start** | 1 Nov 2026 |
| **End** | 30 Nov 2026 |
| **Goal** | Raise employee awareness of email best practices and introduce the new policy for sending emails and opening attachments, to protect the organization from phishing emails and malicious attachments. |

| # | Awareness method | Target audience | Content | Start | End | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Awareness emails | All employees | Publish the new email policy and explain best practices for sending emails, such as checking the recipient and not sending sensitive data without encryption. | 1 Nov 2026 | 5 Nov 2026 | Introduce all employees to the new policy and the rules for sending emails. |
| 2 | Workshop | All employees | How to spot phishing emails and malicious attachments, with real examples of suspicious emails and how to report them. | 8 Nov 2026 | 12 Nov 2026 | Enable employees to tell safe emails from suspicious ones. |
| 3 | Posters and visuals | All employees | Short tips in offices and on company screens: don't open an attachment from an unknown sender, check the sender's address, report any suspicious email. | 1 Nov 2026 | 30 Nov 2026 | Keep reminding employees throughout the campaign. |
| 4 | Phishing simulation | All employees, with a focus on senior management and finance | Send a fake phishing email with an attachment or link, then give direct awareness to anyone who opens it. | 15 Nov 2026 | 22 Nov 2026 | Measure employee awareness and find who needs extra training. |
| 5 | Short quiz and survey | All employees | Questions on the sending policy, opening attachments, and how to spot phishing emails. | 23 Nov 2026 | 30 Nov 2026 | Measure how much employees benefited and improve future campaigns. |

Why this design: the campaign moves from **inform** (emails, posters) to **practice** (workshop) to **test** (phishing simulation) to **measure** (quiz). The simulation focuses on senior management and finance because those are exactly the roles a pay-slip or money-transfer lure, like the one in [Task 02](../task-02-phishing-email-analysis-with-oledump/), is built to target.
